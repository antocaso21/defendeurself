About DefendEUrself

Cybersecurity education built around real life

DefendEUrself strengthens the digital autonomy of adults aged 45 and above through practical learning, culturally relevant examples and clear behavioural response scripts.

Objectives

From awareness to autonomous action

General Objective

To strengthen the cybersecurity competence, confidence and digital autonomy of older adults through evidence-based research, accessible training and practical opportunities for cooperation and peer learning.

Specific Objectives

  • Assess cybersecurity knowledge gaps and educational needs among older adults.
  • Develop an accessible and interactive cybersecurity training course.
  • Transform general awareness into practical and repeatable protective behaviour.
  • Encourage cooperation among learners, educators, younger people, community organisations and policymakers.
  • Support continued participation in banking, healthcare, public administration and communication services without fear or dependency.
The challenge

The Digital Resilience Gap

Older adults are sufficiently connected to be targeted, yet too often lack the defensive habits and recovery pathways required to remain safe and autonomous.

01

High activity, concentrated risk

Banking, healthcare, messaging and public administration are increasingly online, making cybersecurity essential to everyday autonomy.

02

The confidence–competence gap

Knowing that scams exist does not necessarily mean knowing how to verify, pause and respond safely under pressure.

03

Dependence on informal support

Many users rely on younger relatives after something has gone wrong, rather than building their own practical digital defence skills.

04

Generic and technical guidance

Existing materials are often abstract, jargon-heavy or disconnected from the platforms and institutions learners actually encounter.

05

Missing recovery pathways

Cybersecurity education rarely provides accessible, shame-free steps for recovering after fraud, data loss or account compromise.

06

Forced digitalisation

When physical alternatives disappear, digital participation becomes necessary for maintaining access to services, rights and administration.

European relevance

A shared European challenge needs a shared response

The project links digital inclusion with usable self-protection. By combining evidence from five national contexts, DefendEUrself develops resources that are comparable across Europe while remaining locally recognisable and culturally relevant.

  • Digital inclusion and participation in essential services
  • Adult learning and active ageing
  • Prevention of online fraud and account compromise
  • Intergenerational learning and community support
  • European cooperation, social inclusion and policy dialogue
“Digital inclusion is incomplete when people can access online services but cannot protect themselves while using them.”
DefendEUrself research framework
Target groups

Designed with learners, educators and communities in mind

Primary target group

Adults aged 45 and above

People with different levels of digital experience, confidence and independence who use online services in their everyday lives.

Secondary target groups

Those who support safer digital participation

Adult educators, training providers, younger people, community organisations, local authorities, policymakers and technology stakeholders.

Work packages

A structured route from research to impact

WP1

Project Management

Coordination, quality assurance, financial management, communication and cooperation among partners.

WP2

DefendEUrself In-Depth Analysis

Cross-national research, national data collection, international analysis, validation and refinement.

WP3

DefendEUrself Training Course

Preparation, content development, evaluation, finalisation, piloting and implementation of the 21 Micro-MOOCs.

WP4

DefendEUrself Network

Platform development, peer discussions, intergenerational hackathons, policy recommendations and sustainability actions.
